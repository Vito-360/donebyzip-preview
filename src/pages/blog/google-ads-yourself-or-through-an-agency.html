Google Ads yourself or through an agency: what pays off for a small business

9 November 2026 · 2 min read · zip

Google doesn’t charge for access, so the real question isn’t what Google Ads costs, but whose time and whose mistakes you pay for.

Google Ads has no monthly subscription or flat fee. You set the budget, and with search ads you pay when someone clicks. How much is sensible to start with is in How much a small business should spend on Google Ads per month.

When doing it yourself makes sense

  • You have one service and one town, so the campaign can stay simple.
  • Your budget is small, and an agency fee would be a large share of the total.
  • You have a few hours a month to check which searches your ads show for and how many enquiries come in.

To start, there are Smart campaigns, Google’s simpler campaign type for small businesses. You link your Google profile, and Google builds the ads and sets the bids. The price of that simplicity is less control: less detailed reports and only one landing page per campaign.

When an agency pays off

  • You have several services or towns and a bigger budget, so every mistake costs more.
  • You don’t know how many calls and enquiries come from the ads, and that needs measuring before anything is optimised.
  • You don’t have time to check the campaign every week.

If you hire an agency, ask for this

  • The ads account is in your name, and you have admin access.
  • A monthly report with cost, clicks and impressions. Google’s rules for partners require that as a minimum, with Google’s cost shown separately from the agency’s fee.
  • A clear agreement on what is measured: calls, messages, forms.
  • A notice period that doesn’t tie you in for months.

If your ads are already spending and the phone isn’t ringing, check the basics first, whoever runs the campaign: Why Google Ads is spending your budget and the phone isn’t ringing.
